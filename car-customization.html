GTA 6 car customization: rims, wheels and every mod explained

Short answer: yes, GTA 6 has full car customization — but there is no single shop that does everything. The work is split across 14 specialty mod shops in Leonida, each with its own focus (lowriders, tuners, off-road, retro, bikes and more). The mod menu has six categories — Pro Builds, Cosmetics, Bodywork, Performance, Wheels and Interior — and in Wheels you can change both the rim style and the wheel size. Donk builds on oversized wheels do exist: Rockstar's own editions page describes Rideout Customs as offering "detailed interiors, exquisite rims, and donk stylings" — but that shop is Ultimate Edition only. Standard Edition players still get 12 shops, including Lowballers Customs for lowriders and rims. (Shop list and categories reported by PowerPyx's launch map coverage, GTA Vibes, ShowGamer and SkyCoach, checked 22 November 2026; the two Ultimate Edition shops are Confirmed from Rockstar's editions page.)

The big-rims complaint, answered honestly

A complaint doing the rounds since launch says the rim selection is weak — that Vice City's real big-rim culture is missing, and NPCs all drive on plain wheels. Here is what is actually true, piece by piece:

  • Donks ARE in the game — including in traffic. The Albany Manana is a 1970s coupe raised on huge gold-and-chrome rims, and it appears as a street car, not just a showpiece. Lowriders and donks are part of Leonida's traffic mix, reflecting South Florida's real car culture. (Leonida Interactive vehicle database; launch-week player reports)
  • But the headline donk shop is paywalled. Rideout Customs — the shop Rockstar built around lift kits, big wheels and donk builds — only opens for Ultimate Edition owners. If you bought the Standard Edition, the most Miami part of customization is locked. Lowballers Customs (lowriders, rims, interiors) is the closest shop included in every copy.
  • Real rim brands will never appear. Forgiato, Corleone and every other real-world wheel brand are absent by design: GTA has always used fictional brands for cars and parts, and GTA 6 is no different. If you were waiting to see a real Forgiato logo on an NPC car, that was never going to happen — in this game or any GTA.
  • Ordinary traffic variety is the fair part of the complaint. Showcase builds exist, but everyday NPC cars mostly wear stock-style wheels, and the wildest builds cluster around car meets, street takeovers and specific districts rather than every street. That is a design choice players can fairly criticise — it is not a missing feature.

How customization works

Drive a car into any mod shop — they appear on the map as an orange car icon once you have uncovered the area, and the map filter can show only mod shops. Inside, the menu splits into six tabs (L1/R1 on a controller). Every shop sells performance upgrades and visual mods, but specialty shops are required for signature mods: the deepest parts for a vehicle class are only stocked by the shop that specialises in it, so a lowrider build means a trip to Lowballers, not whichever shop is nearest. (GTA Vibes, ShowGamer, HowToVideoGame)

One practical point from our garages guide: register a car before you spend serious money modding it. An unregistered stolen car can be lost permanently — along with every mod on it.

The six mod categories

  • Pro Builds — ready-made full builds and kits, including wheel kits and full retro kits. The Ganado Retro Build for Jason's pickup is the famous one: a custom paintjob, interior and modded engine in a single package from Coastal Auto Care.
  • Cosmetics — resprays, paint finishes, custom and curated liveries, license plates and lights.
  • Bodywork — hoods and hood catches, bumpers, splitters, spoilers, exhausts, window tints and lights.
  • Performance — full engine upgrades, intakes, transmission and boost. You can rev the engine with R2 to hear a part before you buy it, and exhaust and supercharger changes alter the car's sound.
  • Wheels — rim styles, wheel sizes and tyre profiles (covered in detail below).
  • Interior — steering wheels, seats, upholstery materials, colours, stitching and trim.

(SkyCoach, HowToVideoGame)

Wheels and rims: what you can actually change

This is the section the complaints are really about, so here is exactly what the Wheels category does, per hands-on launch guides:

  • Rim style — swap the design of the wheels, with different styles stocked by different specialty shops (Lowballers leans classic lowrider; Rideout leans big and flashy).
  • Wheel size — yes, size is changeable. Upsize the wheels for a lower-profile tyre, or downsize for a taller tyre profile. This is new depth compared to GTA 5, where the wheel was a fixed size per car.
  • Suspension and lift — lowered suspension kits sit under Pro Builds/Wheel Kits, and lift kits at the donk and off-road specialists raise the car — the combination of a lift plus oversized wheels is what makes a true donk build.
  • Finishing touches — Rockstar's footage and descriptions also show coloured lug nuts, underglow and two-tone paint among the visual options.

What we have not seen reliably reported: a total count of rim designs, or per-rim prices. Guides disagree or stay silent, so we are not inventing a number — if a site gives you an exact rim count today, treat it as unverified. (SkyCoach; GTA Vibes)

All 14 mod shops

The full list, by county, from PowerPyx's launch map as reported by GTA Vibes and ShowGamer. Standard Edition owners get 12 of the 14:

  • Coastal Auto Care — Mariana County (the Keys). Retro parts and cosmetics; home of the Ganado Retro Build. Open in every edition.
  • Capri Classic — Mariana County. Muscle cars.
  • Two Wheel Fury Motorsport — Vice-Dale County. Sports bikes.
  • Euroform Automotive — Vice-Dale County. Luxury cars.
  • Vice Auto Customs — Vice-Dale County. Tuners.
  • Lowballers Customs — Vice-Dale County. Lowriders, rims and interiors — the big-rim stop for Standard Edition players.
  • Rideout Customs — Vice-Dale County. Donks and big wheels. Ultimate Edition only (Confirmed, Rockstar editions page).
  • Vice Vinyl — Vice-Dale County. Wraps and liveries.
  • Stockyard Speedshop — Vice-Dale County. Hot rods.
  • Whisler Motorsport — Kelly County. Racing cars.
  • Dirt Merchants — Kelly County. Dirt bikes.
  • Wasteland Off-Road — Kelly County. Off-road cars and trucks.
  • One-Eyed Willie's Auto Shop — Leonard County, Lake Leonida. Off-road builds and hand-painted art. Ultimate Edition only (Confirmed, Rockstar editions page).
  • Ledford Motorcycle Workshop — Lummox County. Choppers and classic American motorcycles.

Pay 'n' Spray is not a mod shop

Do not confuse the two. Rockstar North's Rob Nelson told Famitsu that Pay 'n' Spray handles repairs and a quick colour change — "you can basically only change the color there" — while elaborate paint jobs and all real modding happen at tuning/mod shops. Pay 'n' Spray still matters for the wanted system and for registering tracked cars (see our garages and registration guide), but it will not build your car. (HowToVideoGame, reporting the Famitsu interview; SkyCoach)

FAQ

Can you change rim size in GTA 6?
Yes. In the Wheels category at a mod shop you can change the rim style and the wheel size: go bigger for a low-profile tyre look, or smaller for a taller tyre profile. Lift kits and suspension changes at the right specialty shops take it further, up to full donk builds with oversized wheels.

Where do you customize cars in GTA 6?
At mod shops spread across Leonida — 14 in total, each with a specialty such as lowriders, tuners, off-road or retro builds. Every shop handles the basics, but signature mods for a vehicle class are only sold by the shop that specializes in it. Pay 'n' Spray only does repairs and a basic colour change.

Are there donks and big rims in GTA 6?
Yes. Rockstar itself describes Rideout Customs as offering detailed interiors, exquisite rims and donk stylings, and donk-style cars such as the Albany Manana appear in traffic. Two honest limits: Rideout Customs is exclusive to the Ultimate Edition, and every wheel in the game is a fictional brand — real-world names like Forgiato or Corleone never appear in GTA.
