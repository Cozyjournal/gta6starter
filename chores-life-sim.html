GTA 6 household chores and life-sim features: what's actually in the game

Short answer: yes, the chores are real — GTA 6 genuinely asks you to take out the garbage, repair and paint Jason's house, unclog a sink, eat, sleep, shower and work out — but almost all of it is optional. The chores feed two systems: a body system (Health, Stamina and Focus buffs from food, rest and exercise) and a relationship system with Lucia, where errands marked "Lucia will appreciate this" earn hearts. Skipping the house chores before Lucia's release costs you one starting relationship heart — nothing more. Rockstar North's Rob Nelson has said the romance can be nurtured, neglected or skipped entirely, and that it carries no particular gameplay benefit. (GameSpot's hands-on tips, Polygon, Sportskeeda, Output Lag and IGN's Nelson interviews, checked 22 November 2026.)

How the "chore simulator" argument started

One screenshot did most of the damage: Jason in his kitchen, holding a bin bag, with the prompt "Hold X to take out the garbage" on screen. It spread as proof that Rockstar had turned GTA into a chore simulator — and the clips that followed (painting walls, fixing the roof, building flat-pack furniture with Lucia) poured fuel on it. Streamers coined the insults, defenders answered that the tasks are optional and take minutes, and the argument has run since launch day.

Both sides are working from real footage. What follows is the verified list of what is actually in the game, what it does, and what happens if you ignore it — so you can judge the label for yourself.

The household chores that are actually in the game

These are the domestic tasks confirmed across launch coverage and hands-on guides — not rumours:

  • Taking out the garbage — the famous prompt. Safehouses are deeply interactive: you can throw trash in the bin and use the appliances, and the garbage run is part of settling into Jason's house. (Vice's open-world feature reporting, via Game Rant; Vice Assist's gameplay file)
  • House repairs — before Lucia comes home, Jason can fix the front steps, the leaky roof, a railing and an interior wall, then paint the wall. The repairs are free, one-time jobs placed in the opening hours, and Lucia comments on them when she moves in — she notices the fixed steps no longer squeak. (USA TODAY's launch coverage; Sportskeeda; Output Lag)
  • Unclogging a sink — a plumbing task appears inside a rent-collection job: a tenant withholds overdue rent until the sink works, so Jason fixes it. This one is part of a mission rather than free-roam upkeep. (Technobezz, reporting streamer playthroughs; launch-week player clips)
  • Building furniture together — player clips from the opening hours show Jason and Lucia assembling a flat-pack (IKEA-style) table at home. Reported from gameplay footage; treat the exact scope as still emerging. (Launch-week player clips)
  • Grooming — at the bathroom mirror you can shave and tidy up; hair can be cut shorter at the safehouse, while growing it back out needs a barber. (Vice's feature reporting, via Game Rant)
  • The fridge — the safehouse fridge holds specific snacks that give temporary stat boosts (protein shakes and food can push stats past 100% temporarily). You eat food you've bought or stocked — full home cooking is a different question, covered below. (Vice Assist; GameSpot)

Eat, sleep, shower, work out: the body system

The chores sit on top of a genuine simulation layer. Jason and Lucia have three core stats — Health, Stamina and Focus (Focus is the slow-time aiming ability) — and daily care moves all three:

  • Eating, sleeping and showering give temporary buffs to the stats, lasting for your session or until the character dies. Activities show which stat they touch: a pink heart for Health, blue lightning for Stamina, a purple eye for Focus. (GameSpot's hands-on tips; USA TODAY)
  • Exercise builds stats permanently — lifting weights grows Health and visible muscle, cardio like running, biking and kayaking grows Stamina, and calm activities such as fishing and pool grow Focus. Gains need regular upkeep to maintain. (GameSpot; Vice Assist)
  • The gym is a system, not a cutscene — gyms sell paid memberships, workouts are trigger-button mini-games, and a fitness app on the phone tracks each character's progress. Go together and both Jason and Lucia get the benefit, whichever one you're playing. (GameSpot; USA TODAY)
  • Neglect is visible, not fatal — sleep deprivation gives characters dark circles; injuries leave black eyes and scraped knees. GameSpot's verdict: neglecting the routine "won't ruin the game, it will certainly put you at a disadvantage." (GameSpot; launch review coverage)
  • Cars need care too — vehicles run on fuel and need refuelling, which is part of why reviewers describe the game as something you live in rather than just rampage through. (NPR's review; launch review coverage)

The relationship chores: "Lucia will appreciate this"

The tasks that triggered the loudest complaints are the six Chapter 1 jobs marked with a pink heart and the line "Lucia will appreciate this", offered while Jason waits for Lucia's release from prison:

  • Fix your car — take Jason's Ganado to Coastal Auto Care for a repair and fresh paint (this is the mission Just a Scratch; the first repair is free).
  • Buy flowers — ordered at a flower stand and delivered to the house; you carry nothing.
  • Pick up groceries — buy them at the marked store and bring them home; the bag can ride in your trunk.
  • Order a necklace — from the jeweller; delivery takes a couple of in-game days, and Lucia finds it when she moves in.
  • Get a haircut — a fresh cut at the barber, flagged on the map like the others.
  • The house repairs — the steps, roof, railing and wall from the list above.

Doing them raises the relationship level, shown on the Jason & Lucia pause-menu tab as a named level out of four (for example, "Crazy in Love (3/4)"), with a heart flashing when it changes. After her release, time together keeps it growing — beach walks, cycling, the gym, pool, mini-golf, kayaking, or simply robbing stores together — and answering her messages matters: ignoring a goodnight text "may impact your relationship a bit." (Output Lag's relationship guide; Edge's cover story and Rob Nelson's IGN/Famitsu interviews, via GTA BOOM and GTA6Map)

So is any of it mandatory? What does skipping cost?

Almost nothing is mandatory. The six appreciation tasks are optional map jobs — none of them costs or gates a mission — and Rockstar designed the romance to be skippable: Nelson told IGN the pair can end up "as a couple or simply as accomplices," and players who skip the romance lose some interactions (holding hands, kissing) that can be won back later. The concrete penalties reported for skipping are small:

  • Skip the pre-release chores and Lucia comes home to a lower starting relationship — guides report one heart less at the start of "Coming Home." Annoying, recoverable, and "not a huge difference" to gameplay. (Sportskeeda; Output Lag)
  • Skip the body routine and you simply play without the temporary stat buffs and let permanent gym gains decay — a disadvantage, never a fail state. (GameSpot)
  • There is no breakup mechanic. Lucia cannot leave Jason, there is no infidelity storyline you can trigger, and the two remain partners in crime whatever you do. (Sportskeeda; Output Lag)

Nelson has also been explicit that keeping them close brings no particular gameplay benefit — but that whether you nurtured the relationship "will definitely have a bearing on how their story plays out." In other words: scenes and tone, not stats or unlocks. (IGN interview, via GTA BOOM/GTA6Map)

The debate, fairly: what each side gets right

The critics are right that the domestic layer is front and centre in the opening hours, not buried in a menu — Rockstar put the repairs and errands in front of every player, some of the most-shared launch footage is literally housework, and a game people waited 13 years for opening with chores is a fair target for jokes. The relationship errands are also genuinely new territory for GTA, and not every player wants a romance meter in their crime game.

The defenders are right that nearly all of it is optional and quick: the repairs are one-time, minutes-long jobs, the appreciation tasks can be ignored for the price of one heart, and the whole routine is the direct descendant of Red Dead Redemption 2's camp chores and Arthur's upkeep — a lineage reviewers keep citing. NPR's reviewer, after noting "yes, you have to sleep, yes, you have to eat," admitted he ended up wanting to do it because it felt like roleplaying. And whatever the memes say, the game around the chores launched to a 99 Metascore — the mayhem is still there; the chores are the breathing space between it. (Polygon; NPR via launch coverage; USA TODAY)

What we could not verify

Honesty section, as always: a few claims doing the rounds are not backed by two reliable sources, so they are not stated as fact above. Cooking full meals at home — the verified version is fridge snacks and bought food; Rockstar reportedly experimented with making players buy groceries and decided that went too far. Washing dishes as a repeatable chore appears in player posts but not in two credible outlets we could check. And while the relationship state affects how scenes play out, no credible source reports a different ending caused by skipping chores — anyone claiming a "bad ending for lazy players" is guessing. If verified reporting lands, this page will be updated. (Game Rant, reporting Vice's feature coverage)

FAQ

Do you have to do chores in GTA 6?
Almost none of them are mandatory. The house repairs, garbage and relationship errands are optional ambient tasks: Rockstar North's Rob Nelson has said the relationship system can be nurtured, neglected or skipped entirely. Skipping the pre-release house chores means Jason and Lucia start one relationship heart lower when she comes home — a small penalty, not a failed mission. The body-care loop (eat, sleep, shower, work out) is also optional, but neglecting it leaves your Health, Stamina and Focus buffs on the table.

What chores are actually in GTA 6?
Verified in launch coverage: taking out the garbage and throwing trash in the bin at the safehouse, repairing Jason's house (front steps, leaky roof, railing, interior wall) and painting it, unclogging a sink during a rent-collection job, shaving and grooming at the bathroom mirror, and grabbing stat-boosting snacks from the fridge. On top of the chores sit the relationship tasks marked "Lucia will appreciate this" — flowers, groceries, a necklace, a haircut and the house repairs.

Do chores affect the story or endings in GTA 6?
Chores feed the relationship meter, and Rockstar says the state of Jason and Lucia's relationship has a bearing on how their story plays out — but there is no breakup or infidelity mechanic, the couple stay partners whatever you do, and Rockstar has said there is no particular gameplay benefit to keeping them close. No credible source reports a different ending caused by skipping chores.
