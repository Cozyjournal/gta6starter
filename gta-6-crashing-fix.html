GTA 6 crashing? How to fix error CE-108255-1 on PS5

Short answer: there is no confirmed fix yet for GTA 6's CE-108255-1 crash (checked 21 November 2026). The code is Sony's generic "an error occurred with the game/application" message — the game itself crashed. Players report it at the opening prologue and in the early Pipework mission, sometimes at the exact same point every time. Rockstar has published no patch, statement or known-issues note, and its support page sends console errors to PlayStation or Xbox support. Clearing the cache, rebuilding the database, reinstalling and even factory resetting have not fixed it for the players quoted by Dexerto and Polygon — so do not wipe your console for this. What you can do is below: install every update first, protect your progress, and report the crash so Rockstar can fix it.

What CE-108255-1 means

CE-108255-1 is not a GTA 6 code. It is a general PlayStation 5 error: Sony's support page describes it as "an error occurred with the game/application" and gives no specific cause. When you see it, the game crashed and the console closed it back to the home screen. It does not mean your PSN account or your network is at fault, and on its own it does not mean your console is broken. (Confirmed from Sony's error-code page, via Dexerto and Polygon.)

The same code has appeared at other games' launches before. What makes it a GTA 6 story is the pattern: many players crash at the same story points, again and again, which points to the game rather than to individual consoles.

Where the crash happens

  • The opening prologue. Several players crash at the exact same point during the opening prologue, including at least one PS5 Pro owner who has been unable to play since launch. (Reported by Dexerto, 20 November 2026)
  • The Pipework mission. One player reports the game crashes every time they use the plunger or stand near the sink in Pipework, an early Chapter 1 mission where Mandy is introduced — and they can no longer progress through the story. (Reported by Dexerto, 20 November 2026)
  • Randomly, hours in. Other players get hours into the game before the first crash, then crash repeatedly — one report says after about 15 minutes of play, another after about two hours. (Reported by Dexerto and Polygon)
  • Which consoles: reports name base, Digital and Slim PS5 models, and at least one PS5 Pro. GamesRadar's own reviewer has not seen the crash, so it does not affect everyone. (Reported by GamesRadar, via The Droid Guy)
  • Xbox too. Some Xbox Series X players report crashes as well — one after only 15 minutes — but no Xbox error code has been reported so far. (Reported by Dexerto, 20 November 2026)

What to try, in order

Work through these in order. They are the steps Sony and the launch guides agree on — but be honest with yourself about what they can do: for the players quoted by Dexerto, none of steps 1–6 fixed a crash that repeats at the same story point. If your crash repeats at the same point, the realistic outcome is waiting for a Rockstar patch.

1. Install the GTA 6 update. This is Sony's official advice for the code, and the fix players report most. GTA 6 got a pre-launch update, version 01.002.001, about 602 MB on PS5 (about 1.6 GB on Xbox). If your copy came from a preload, it may not have installed yet. On PS5, highlight GTA 6 on the home screen, press Options and choose Check for Update. (Reported by HappyGamer and 2ndShift, checked 21 November 2026)

2. Install the required PS5 system software. GTA 6 needs system software 26.07-14.23.00 to run. Go to Settings, then System, then System Software, and install any update. (Reported by 2ndShift, checked 21 November 2026)

3. Restart the console properly. Turn the PS5 fully off and back on — not rest mode. Then test the game at the same crash point.

4. Try the resolution workaround some players report. Polygon reports some players were able to work around the crash by changing their PS5's resolution from Manual to 1080p in the system settings. This is a player-reported workaround from one outlet, not a confirmed fix — but it costs nothing to try.

5. Clear the cache and rebuild the database. This does not delete your games or saves. Turn the PS5 off completely, hold the power button until you hear the second beep (about 7 seconds) to start Safe Mode, connect the controller with a USB cable, press the PS button, then choose Clear Cache and Rebuild Database. (Sony's standard steps for this code, via RP Base)

6. Reinstall — only as a last resort. Deleting GTA 6 keeps your saved data on the console, and a reinstall means downloading about 212 GB again. Players quoted by Dexerto reinstalled — some three times — and still crashed at the same point, so only do this if your crash looks like a bad install rather than a repeatable story-point crash. Back up saves to the cloud first if you have PS Plus.

7. Report it. When the PS5 asks, send the error report — Sony passes crash reports to the developer. Note the exact spot where it crashes and send that to Rockstar Support too: that is how a game bug gets fixed. Save by hand before and after missions meanwhile, so a crash costs you less progress. (Reported by RP Base and The Droid Guy)

What NOT to do

  • Do not factory reset your PS5. Players quoted by Dexerto factory reset their consoles and the crash came straight back. A reset erases everything on the console and has not fixed this for anyone reported so far.
  • Do not pay for a repair yet. Unless other games crash with the same code too, there is no evidence your console is faulty — hold off on a paid repair until Rockstar or Sony says more. (Reported by The Droid Guy)
  • Do not keep reinstalling. If the crash repeats at the same story point after one reinstall, more downloads will not change it. The game needs a patch, not another 212 GB.
  • Do not delete your saved data. Nothing in the reported fixes requires it, and losing progress to a troubleshooting step is the worst outcome here.

CE-107891-6 — the install error

A different code, a different problem: CE-107891-6 means the game or one of its updates did not install properly. Sony's fix for this one is straightforward — delete GTA 6 in Settings, then Storage, and install it again. Your saved data stays on the console. If you hit this code during install, do not patch over it: delete and redo the install. (Reported by RP Base; the code also appears in our preload not working guide.)

What about the cause?

Neither Rockstar nor Sony has named a cause, confirmed a link to the game, or published a count of affected players. GameSpot reports Rockstar Support sent at least one affected player to Sony's support team, and that the problem does not appear to be very widespread. Some players blame ageing PS5 cooling — the "liquid metal" theory — but that is an unproven theory, not a confirmed cause, and it does not explain crashes on PS5 Pro or Xbox. Gizmodo reported PlayStation support told at least one person the problem is a software issue, which would point back at Rockstar. Until one of the two companies says so officially, the cause is Not announced. (Reported by Polygon, Dexerto and 2ndShift, checked 21 November 2026)

As of the morning of 21 November 2026, no new game update has come out since launch and Rockstar has still not posted any GTA 6 known issues, patch notes or statement about the crashes. We will update this page when a patch or an official fix appears.

FAQ

Is there a fix for GTA 6 error CE-108255-1 yet?
No confirmed fix as of 21 November 2026. Rockstar has published no patch, statement or known-issues note for it, and Sony's only advice is to update the game. Clearing the cache, rebuilding the database, reinstalling and even factory resetting have not fixed it for the players quoted by Dexerto and Polygon.

Will reinstalling GTA 6 fix the CE-108255-1 crash?
Probably not. Players quoted by Dexerto reinstalled the game — some three times — and still crashed at the same point. Reinstalling also means downloading about 212 GB again, so try the update and system-software checks first.

Does CE-108255-1 mean my PS5 is broken?
Not on its own. CE-108255-1 is Sony's generic code for "an error occurred with the game/application" — it means the game crashed, not that the console is faulty. If other games crash with the same code too, that points to the console; if only GTA 6 does, it points to the game, and only a Rockstar update is likely to fix it.
